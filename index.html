Why copy from the image instead of retyping

Retyping works for a three-word label. It fails for a dense invoice, a multi-line chat screenshot, or a product label with serial numbers. Typos creep in, line breaks disappear, and you burn minutes every time the same job shows up again.

An on-image copy tool runs OCR under the hood, then lays an invisible text layer on the picture so you can select and copy just what you need. Full-page extract is still available when you want everything at once.

How to copy text from a picture online, step by step

  1. Start with a clear source file. JPG, PNG, JPEG, WEBP, BMP, and PDF pages work. Prefer the highest-resolution export or scan you have; soft phone photos and heavy compression make characters harder to read.

  4. Or copy the whole block. Use copy-all or the reading-order result when you need the full page, then paste into your editor.
  5. Tidy brand names and numbers if needed. You are editing plain text now — you are not locked inside the pixels.
  6. Repeat for the next shot. Separate clear uploads beat one blurry collage.

Tips that improve selection accuracy

  • Shoot or export straight-on when you can. Extreme tilt warps letter shapes and raises misreads.
  • Prefer the original file over a screenshot of a screenshot. Each re-save softens edges the recognizer needs.
  • Give the tool enough contrast. Light gray text on a light background fails more often than dark type on a clean page.
  • Crop to the text region on busy photos. A desk photo with a small sticky note is harder than a tight crop of the note.
  • Match the language when the page is not English. Supported languages include English, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Russian, Thai, and Hindi.
